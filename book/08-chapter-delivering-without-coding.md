# Chapter 8: Delivering Without Coding a Single Line

## The Three Delivery Methods

You've closed the deal. The deposit is in your bank account. Now you need to deliver a website that makes your client say "WOW."

And you're going to do it without touching a single line of code.

There are three ways to deliver websites in this business. Each has its strengths, and most successful sellers use a combination of all three depending on the project.

## Option 1: Fiverr Developers — Hiring a $150 Dev to Build a $3,000 Site

This is the bread and butter of the business. Here's exactly how to do it.

### Finding Great Developers

Go to Fiverr and search for:
- "WordPress website"
- "business website design"
- "Wix website"
- "Squarespace website"
- "responsive website"

**What to look for in a developer:**

| Criteria | Minimum | Ideal |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Star rating | 4.7+ | 4.9+ |
| Number of reviews | 50+ | 200+ |
| Seller level | Level 2 | Top Rated |
| Turnaround time | 7 days | 3-5 days |
| Response time | 24 hours | Under 2 hours |
| Revisions included | 2+ | Unlimited |
| Portfolio quality | Good | Excellent |

### How to Brief Your Developer

The quality of the website depends heavily on how well you brief the developer. Here's a template I use for every project:

> **Project Brief for [Client Business Name]**
>
> **Business Type:** [e.g., Italian restaurant]
> **Location:** [e.g., Dallas, TX]
> **Website Goal:** [e.g., Attract new customers, display menu, enable reservations]
>
> **Pages Needed:**
> 1. Homepage — Hero section with business name, tagline, and call-to-action
> 2. About — Story of the business, team photos
> 3. Services/Menu — [Specific to business]
> 4. Gallery — Photos of [products/location/work]
> 5. Contact — Address, phone, email, contact form, Google Map
>
> **Must-Have Features:**
> - Mobile responsive
> - Click-to-call phone button
> - Contact form that sends to [client email]
> - Google Maps embed
> - Social media links
>
> **Design Style:** [Modern and clean / Bold and colorful / Elegant and minimal]
> **Color Preferences:** [Client's brand colors or "designer's choice"]
> **Reference Sites:** [1-2 websites the client likes]
>
> **Content Provided:**
> - Logo: [attached / client will provide]
> - Photos: [attached / client will provide / use stock photos]
> - Text content: [attached / developer to write placeholder text]
>
> **Deadline:** [Date] — need first draft in [X] days
>
> **Budget:** $[Your offer to the developer]

### The Development Process

**Day 1: Send the Brief**
- Message your developer with the brief and all materials
- Confirm timeline and price
- Place the Fiverr order

**Day 2-4: Developer Works**
- They build the site
- Good developers will send a progress update or screenshot midway
- If they have questions, answer promptly (delays on your end = delays on theirs)

**Day 4-5: First Draft Review**
- Developer sends you a preview link
- Review EVERY page on both desktop and mobile
- Check: spelling, images, links, contact form, load speed, mobile layout
- Make a clear, numbered list of revisions

**Day 5-6: Revisions**
- Send your revision list to the developer
- Be specific: "On the homepage, move the phone number above the menu button" NOT "It needs to look better"
- Most developers include 2-3 rounds of revisions

**Day 7: Final Review**
- Check everything one more time
- Test the contact form (send a test message)
- Check on different devices (your phone, tablet, laptop)
- Once satisfied, approve the delivery on Fiverr

**Day 7-10: Client Presentation**
- Send the client a preview link or screenshots
- Schedule a quick call: "Let me walk you through your new website!"
- Note any changes the client requests
- Send revision requests to developer
- Deliver final version

### Pro Tips for Working with Fiverr Developers

**1. Build a roster of 3-5 trusted developers.** Don't rely on one person. People get sick, go on vacation, or get overwhelmed. Having backups means you never miss a deadline.

**2. Always test with a small project first.** Before giving a developer a $3,000 client project, pay them $100-$150 for a sample project. This lets you evaluate their speed, quality, and communication.

**3. Communicate through Fiverr's messaging system.** This protects both of you. If there's a dispute, Fiverr can see the conversation history.

**4. Tip good developers.** A $20-$50 tip goes a long way. Developers who feel appreciated will prioritize your projects and go the extra mile.

**5. Pay for "extra fast" delivery when needed.** Most developers offer rush delivery for an additional fee. If a client is eager, it's worth paying $30-$50 extra to deliver in 3 days instead of 7.

## Option 2: No-Code Tools — Wix, Squarespace, WordPress Templates

Sometimes you'll want to build the website yourself. Not because you need to — but because for simple sites, it can be faster and more profitable than hiring a developer.

No-code website builders have gotten incredibly good. You can build a professional, beautiful website in 2-4 hours with zero coding knowledge.

### When to Use No-Code

- Simple 1-3 page websites
- Clients with tight budgets ($800-$1,500)
- When you want 100% of the profit (no developer cost)
- When you want the fastest possible delivery

### The Top No-Code Platforms

**Wix**
- Best for: Beautiful templates, drag-and-drop ease
- Cost: $17-$32/month (client pays this)
- Learning curve: Very easy — 1-2 hours to learn
- Best templates: Restaurant, salon, portfolio, small business

**Squarespace**
- Best for: Sleek, modern designs; creative businesses
- Cost: $16-$33/month (client pays this)
- Learning curve: Easy — 2-3 hours to learn
- Best templates: Photography, creative services, retail

**WordPress + Elementor**
- Best for: Maximum flexibility, SEO
- Cost: $0 for WordPress + $0-$59/year for Elementor + $3-$10/month hosting
- Learning curve: Moderate — 5-8 hours to learn well
- Best templates: Any industry, most customizable

### How to Build a Wix/Squarespace Site (The Quick Version)

1. Pick a template that matches the client's industry
2. Replace the placeholder text with the client's information
3. Replace placeholder images with the client's photos (or stock photos)
4. Add the client's logo, contact info, and social media links
5. Set up the contact form
6. Preview on mobile and make adjustments
7. Connect the client's domain name
8. Publish

Total time: 2-4 hours
Total cost: $0 (the client pays for their own hosting/subscription)
Your profit: 100% of whatever you charged

### The Hybrid Approach

My favorite strategy is the hybrid approach:
- For complex sites (5+ pages, e-commerce, custom features): use a Fiverr developer
- For simple sites (1-3 pages, basic business site): build it yourself on Wix or Squarespace
- This maximizes profit and gives you flexibility

## Option 3: White-Label Agencies — Hand It Off Entirely

White-label agencies are companies that build websites under YOUR brand name. The client thinks you did everything; the agency does the actual work.

### How It Works

1. You close a deal with a client for $3,000
2. You send the project requirements to your white-label agency
3. They design and build the website (under your brand)
4. You review and deliver to the client
5. They charge you $500-$1,200 depending on complexity

### Where to Find White-Label Agencies

- Search Google for "white label web design services"
- Search Fiverr for "white label website"
- Join Facebook Groups for web agencies and ask for recommendations
- Check platforms like Design Pickle, Jenius, or 99designs

### When to Use White-Label

- When you're scaling and closing more deals than you can manage
- When the project is complex (e-commerce, custom functionality)
- When you want premium quality with zero effort
- When you're ready to operate like a real agency

### Cost Comparison

| Method | Your Cost | Client Pays | Your Profit |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Fiverr developer | $100-$300 | $2,000-$5,000 | $1,700-$4,700 |
| No-code (you build) | $0 | $800-$2,500 | $800-$2,500 |
| White-label agency | $500-$1,200 | $3,000-$8,000 | $2,000-$6,800 |

## Quality Control: Making Sure the Client Is Thrilled

A happy client pays on time, gives referrals, and signs up for monthly maintenance. An unhappy client causes headaches, demands refunds, and leaves bad reviews. Quality control is the difference.

### The 15-Point Quality Checklist

Before you show any website to a client, check every single one of these:

**Design & Visual**
1. Does it look professional and modern?
2. Are fonts consistent and readable?
3. Are colors consistent with the business's brand?
4. Are all images high quality (not pixelated or stretched)?
5. Does it look great on mobile (test on your actual phone)?

**Functionality**
6. Do all links work (no broken links)?
7. Does the contact form submit correctly?
8. Does the phone number work as a click-to-call on mobile?
9. Do social media links open in a new tab?
10. Does the Google Map show the correct location?

**Content**
11. Is all text accurate (business name, address, phone, hours)?
12. No spelling or grammar errors?
13. Are prices/services accurate (if applicable)?
14. Is the "About" section compelling?

**Technical**
15. Does the site load in under 3 seconds?

If ANY of these fail, send it back to the developer for fixes before showing the client. It's much better to delay delivery by a day than to present a site with obvious issues.

### The Client Review Process

When you present the website to the client, follow this process:

**Step 1: Build Excitement**
> "Your website is ready! I'm really happy with how it turned out. Let me walk you through it."

**Step 2: Walk Through Each Page**
Show them every page. Point out features they specifically asked for. Show them the mobile version.

**Step 3: Invite Feedback**
> "What do you think? Is there anything you'd like to change or adjust?"

Give them 2-3 days to review with fresh eyes. Most clients will have a few minor changes — a photo swap, a text edit, a color tweak. That's normal.

**Step 4: Implement Revisions**
Send revision requests to your developer. Minor changes should take 1-2 days.

**Step 5: Final Approval**
> "I've made all the changes you requested. Take one more look and let me know if you're 100% happy. Once you approve, we'll push it live!"

**Step 6: Go Live**
Publish the website and connect the domain name. Send the client a congratulations message:

> "Your website is LIVE! Check it out at [URL]. You can share this with customers, add it to your business cards, and post it on social media. Congratulations!"

**Step 7: Collect Final Payment**
Send the invoice for the remaining 50% balance. Most clients pay within 24 hours when they're excited about their new site.

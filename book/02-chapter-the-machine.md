# Chapter 2: The Machine — How It Works

## The 4-Step System: Lead → Pitch → Close → Deliver

Every successful business runs on a system. McDonald's has a system for making burgers. Amazon has a system for shipping packages. And you're about to have a system for selling websites.

I call it **The Machine**, and it has exactly four steps:

### Step 1: LEAD — Find Someone Who Needs a Website

This is where Fiverr comes in. You're going to buy leads — real contact information of business owners who need websites — from specialized sellers on Fiverr. You can also generate your own leads for free using Google Maps, Facebook Groups, and cold outreach.

Your goal: **get 20-30 new leads per week into your Excel spreadsheet.**

This step costs: $50-$200/week (if buying leads) or $0 (if generating your own).

### Step 2: PITCH — Make Contact and Present the Opportunity

You reach out to each lead with a simple, non-pushy message. You're not selling yet — you're starting a conversation. You ask about their business, their goals, and their current online presence. If they don't have a website (or have a terrible one), you casually mention that you help businesses like theirs get online.

Your goal: **have 5-10 real conversations per week.**

This step costs: $0 (just your time).

### Step 3: CLOSE — Turn the Conversation Into a Deal

This is where the magic happens. You show them examples of websites you've delivered (we'll talk about building your portfolio). You explain how a website will bring them more customers. You give them a price. They say yes. You collect a deposit.

Your goal: **close 1-3 deals per week at $1,500-$5,000 each.**

This step costs: $0.

### Step 4: DELIVER — Outsource and Collect the Rest

You hop on Fiverr, hire a developer for $100-$300, give them the client's requirements, review the work, and deliver it to the client. Client is happy, you collect the remaining balance, and you move on to the next deal.

Your goal: **deliver every website within 7-14 days.**

This step costs: $100-$300 per project.

---

That's the entire business model. Four steps, running on a loop, forever. The more leads you put in, the more money comes out.

Let's do some quick math:

| Input | Weekly | Monthly |
|-------|--------|---------|
| New leads | 25 | 100 |
| Conversations | 8 | 32 |
| Deals closed | 2 | 8 |
| Revenue per deal | $2,500 | $2,500 |
| Dev cost per deal | $200 | $200 |
| **Profit per deal** | **$2,300** | **$2,300** |
| **Monthly profit** | — | **$18,400** |

Even if you cut those numbers in half — you're still making $9,200 per month. And this doesn't include recurring revenue from monthly maintenance packages (more on that later).

## Your Only Job: Talk to People and Manage a Spreadsheet

I want to make this crystal clear because it's the most important mindset shift in this entire book:

**You are not in the website business. You are in the people business.**

Your job description has exactly two line items:

1. **Talk to business owners** who need help getting online
2. **Manage your Excel spreadsheet** so nobody falls through the cracks

That's it. Everything else — the design, the development, the hosting, the technical setup — is handled by someone else. Your Fiverr developer builds the site. Your hosting provider keeps it online. Your client provides the content (photos, text, logo). Your spreadsheet tells you who to call and when.

You are the orchestrator. The conductor. The CEO of a tiny, insanely profitable company with a team of contractors who do the heavy lifting.

### Why the Spreadsheet Matters

I've seen people try to run this business from memory or sticky notes. They always fail. Not because the business model doesn't work, but because they lose track of leads, forget to follow up, and let money slip through the cracks.

Your Excel spreadsheet is your **command center**. It's where you know:
- Who you've contacted and who you haven't
- Who said "maybe" and needs a follow-up on Thursday
- Who said yes and needs a contract sent
- Which projects are in progress and who's waiting for delivery
- Who's paying monthly maintenance and when it's due

Without the spreadsheet, you're guessing. With the spreadsheet, you're running a machine.

We'll set up this spreadsheet in detail in Chapter 5, complete with a template you can copy and use immediately.

## Why This Beats Every Other Side Hustle

I've tried a lot of things. I've done dropshipping. I've sold on Amazon. I've tried affiliate marketing, crypto, stock options, and about fifteen other "opportunities" that promised the moon and delivered nothing.

Here's why selling websites crushes all of them:

### 1. High Margins
You're selling something for $2,000-$5,000 that costs $150-$300 to produce. That's a 90%+ profit margin. Try getting that with dropshipping (10-20% margins) or Amazon FBA (15-30% margins).

### 2. Low Startup Cost
You need a phone, a laptop, and $50-$200 for your first batch of leads. That's it. No inventory, no warehouse, no equipment, no storefront.

### 3. Recurring Revenue Potential
Every client you deliver a website to is a candidate for monthly maintenance at $99-$299/month. After 20-30 clients, you're making $3,000-$6,000/month in recurring revenue — money that comes in whether you work or not.

### 4. Infinite Demand
There are millions of businesses that need websites. New businesses open every single day. Existing businesses need redesigns every few years. The demand never runs out.

### 5. No Technical Ceiling
Since you're not building the websites yourself, you never hit a wall where you "can't do something." Need a complex e-commerce site? There's a Fiverr developer for that. Need a booking system? There's a developer for that. Need a multilingual site? Developer. You can say yes to almost any project.

### 6. Location Independence
You can run this business from your kitchen table, a coffee shop, a beach in Mexico, or a coworking space in Bali. All you need is your laptop and a phone.

### 7. Scalability
When you're ready to grow, you don't need a bigger office or more equipment. You just buy more leads, hire a virtual assistant to help with outreach, and add more developers to your team. The machine scales beautifully.

## The Mindset Shift: From "I Can't Do That" to "I Connect People"

The biggest obstacle for most people starting this business isn't the leads, the spreadsheet, or the phone calls. It's the voice in their head saying:

*"But I don't know how to build websites. Isn't this... dishonest?"*

No. And here's why.

When you go to a restaurant, the manager didn't cook your food. The hostess didn't grow the vegetables. The owner might not know the difference between basil and parsley. But the restaurant provides a valuable service because it brings everything together — the chef, the ingredients, the ambiance, the service — into one experience.

You are building a restaurant for website services. You bring together:
- The client (who has the money and the need)
- The developer (who has the skill)
- The platform (your spreadsheet, your process, your follow-up)

You are the bridge between supply and demand. You are solving a real problem for real people. And you deserve to be compensated for that.

Every single industry in the world has middlemen who add value by connecting, coordinating, and ensuring quality. You're about to become one of them in the most profitable corner of the internet.

# Chapter 1: The Middleman Goldmine

## Why Businesses Pay $2,000–$10,000 for Websites That Cost $200 to Build

Let me tell you something that will either make you angry or excited. Probably both.

A local dentist in Dallas just paid $4,500 for a 5-page website. The website has a homepage, an about page, a services page, a gallery, and a contact form. It was built using a WordPress template that costs $59. The developer who actually built it — a talented freelancer in Pakistan — was paid $200 through Fiverr. The whole thing took about 6 hours to complete.

The person who collected the $4,500? A 28-year-old former bartender who learned this business model eight months ago. She doesn't know how to code. She's never opened a code editor. She didn't even install WordPress.

She made $4,300 in profit from a single deal.

**How is this possible?**

Because the dentist wasn't paying for code. He wasn't paying for a WordPress template. He was paying for:

1. **Someone to handle it for him.** He's busy pulling teeth all day. He doesn't have time to figure out Wix or interview developers.

2. **Trust.** She showed up to his office, looked professional, showed him examples of beautiful websites, and said, "I'll handle everything."

3. **Peace of mind.** He paid once, got a beautiful website, and never had to think about it again.

4. **Perceived value.** A professional website can bring a dentist 5-10 new patients per month. At $500+ per patient, that website pays for itself in the first week.

This is the fundamental truth of this business: **you are not selling a website. You are selling a solution.** And solutions are worth 10x to 50x more than the raw cost of production.

Think about it this way — when you go to a restaurant and pay $18 for a plate of pasta, do you care that the ingredients cost $2.50? No. You're paying for the experience, the convenience, the atmosphere, and the fact that someone else cooked it for you.

Website sales work exactly the same way.

## The Real Skill: Selling, Not Coding

Here's what nobody in the tech industry wants you to know: **the money is in the selling, not the building.**

I've seen brilliant developers who can build anything — complex web apps, beautiful designs, flawless code — who make $40,000 a year. And I've seen smooth-talking salespeople who can't spell "HTML" clearing $200,000 a year selling those developers' work.

The difference? The developer has a technical skill. The salesperson has a business skill. And in the real world, business skills pay more than technical skills almost every time.

Here's what you need to be good at in this business:

**1. Finding people who need websites** — We're going to use Fiverr to buy leads, scrape Google Maps for local businesses, and tap into Facebook Groups. This takes about 30 minutes a day once you have a system.

**2. Having a conversation** — Not a technical presentation. Not a sales pitch from a script (although I'll give you one). Just a normal conversation where you ask questions and listen. "What does your business do? Do you have a website? What would it mean for your business to have a professional online presence?"

**3. Managing a spreadsheet** — Your Excel tracker is your entire business brain. Every lead, every call, every follow-up, every deal — it all lives in one spreadsheet. If you can use Excel at a basic level, you can run this business.

**4. Coordinating delivery** — You send your Fiverr developer the client's requirements, they build it, you review it, you deliver it. That's it. You're a project manager, not a developer.

That's four skills. None of them require a degree, a certification, or any technical knowledge whatsoever.

## How I Accidentally Stumbled Into This Business

I want to tell you a quick story about how this whole thing started.

A friend of mine owns a small landscaping company. One day over beers, he complained that his "website guy" disappeared and his site was broken. He asked if I knew anyone who could help.

I didn't build websites at the time. But I knew that people on Fiverr did. So I told him, "I'll take care of it. I'll charge you $1,500 and have it done in a week."

He said yes without blinking.

I went on Fiverr, found a developer with great reviews, and paid $175 for a stunning landscaping website. The developer delivered it in three days. I sent it to my friend, he loved it, and I pocketed $1,325.

The whole thing took me about two hours of actual work — messaging the developer, sending my friend's logo and photos, and reviewing the final product.

I remember sitting there thinking, "Wait... that actually just happened?"

The next week, my friend referred me to another business owner. Then another. Within a month, I had five clients and had made over $8,000. I still hadn't written a single line of code.

That's when I realized this wasn't a one-time fluke. It was a **business model.** And it could scale.

## The Middleman Advantage

Some people hear this and say, "But isn't that dishonest? You're just a middleman!"

Let me ask you something. Is a real estate agent dishonest? They don't build houses. They don't own the properties. They connect buyers with sellers and take a commission. Nobody calls them dishonest — they call them professionals.

Is a general contractor dishonest? They don't personally lay the bricks, install the plumbing, or wire the electricity. They manage the project and coordinate specialists. Nobody questions their value.

You are doing the exact same thing. You are:

- **Finding clients** who need websites (marketing)
- **Understanding their needs** and presenting solutions (consulting)
- **Coordinating developers** to deliver the work (project management)
- **Ensuring quality** before delivery (quality assurance)
- **Providing ongoing support** and maintenance (client services)

You're not a middleman. You're a **business owner** who provides a valuable service. The client gets a professional website without the headache of finding and managing a developer. The developer gets consistent work without having to find clients. And you get paid for making the whole thing happen.

Everybody wins.

## What This Business Looks Like Day-to-Day

Let me paint a picture of what a typical day looks like once you're up and running:

**Morning (30 minutes):**
- Open your Excel spreadsheet
- Check which leads need follow-up today
- Send 3-5 follow-up emails or texts

**Midday (1 hour):**
- Review any new leads that came in from Fiverr
- Add them to your spreadsheet
- Make 5-10 outreach calls or send personalized messages

**Afternoon (30 minutes):**
- Check in on any active projects with your Fiverr developers
- Review any delivered websites before sending to clients
- Send updates to active clients

**Total time: about 2 hours per day.**

That's it. Two hours a day to build a business that can generate $5,000 to $25,000 per month. And most of that time is spent talking to people and updating a spreadsheet.

Compare that to:
- A 9-to-5 job: 8+ hours/day, fixed salary, someone else gets rich
- Dropshipping: managing inventory, shipping problems, razor-thin margins
- Amazon FBA: massive upfront investment, complex logistics, constant competition
- Freelance coding: you ARE the bottleneck, trading hours for dollars

This model wins on every level because you're leveraging other people's skills (developers) to serve other people's needs (business owners) and keeping the difference.

Welcome to the middleman goldmine.

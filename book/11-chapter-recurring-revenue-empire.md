# Chapter 11: The Recurring Revenue Empire

## Why One-Time Sales Are a Trap

Selling a website for $3,000 feels incredible. That dopamine hit of closing a deal, seeing the deposit hit your account, delivering a beautiful product — there's nothing like it.

But here's the problem: the next month, you start at zero again.

You closed 5 deals last month for $12,500? Great. What's your revenue this month? Zero. Until you close more deals. You're on a hamster wheel. Running fast, making money, but never building real wealth.

One-time sales are income. Recurring revenue is wealth.

The difference:

| Model | Month 1 | Month 6 | Month 12 |
|-------|---------|---------|----------|
| One-time only | $7,500 | $10,000 | $12,500 |
| One-time + recurring | $7,798 | $11,788 | $17,364 |
| Difference | $298 | $1,788 | $4,864 |

That gap grows every single month. And by month 24, the person building recurring revenue is making $7,000+ more per month than the person who's only doing one-time deals.

Recurring revenue is the difference between "making good money" and "building real wealth."

## Hosting, Maintenance, and Updates: Building the Machine

You already know the basics of monthly maintenance from Chapter 9. Now let's go deeper into how to build this into a wealth-generating machine.

### The Three Revenue Streams

**Stream 1: Website Hosting**
- What you pay: $5-$15/month per site (shared hosting)
- What you charge: $49-$99/month
- Your margin: $34-$94/month per client
- Your effort: Nearly zero (hosting companies handle everything)

Hosting is the purest form of passive income in this business. You're essentially reselling server space at a massive markup, and the client is happy to pay because they don't want to deal with it.

**Pro tip:** Get a reseller hosting account from providers like SiteGround, Cloudways, or A2 Hosting. For $30-$80/month, you can host 25-100+ websites. That means your per-site cost drops to $1-$3 when you have 25+ clients.

With 30 clients paying $49/month for hosting alone: $1,470/month revenue, $30-$80 in costs. **$1,390+ in pure profit every month.**

**Stream 2: Maintenance & Updates**
- What it costs you: 10-15 minutes per site per month
- What you charge: $50-$150/month (bundled with hosting or separate)
- Your effort: Run updates, check for issues, make minor changes

Most months, maintenance means clicking "Update" on a few WordPress plugins and making sure nothing broke. For Wix/Squarespace sites, it's even simpler — those platforms handle updates automatically.

**Stream 3: Content Changes & Support**
- What it costs you: 5-30 minutes per request
- What you charge: Included in higher-tier plans (up to X changes/month)

Clients occasionally need their hours updated, a new menu uploaded, a team photo changed, or a service description modified. These take your developer 5-10 minutes. You can do them yourself or delegate to your VA.

### Reducing Churn

To keep clients paying month after month:

1. **Deliver real value.** Don't just collect money — actually maintain their sites. Send a monthly email: "Here's what we did for your website this month."

2. **Be responsive.** When they email with a request, respond within 24 hours. Quick turnarounds make clients feel valued.

3. **Make switching painful (in a good way).** If their website is hosted on YOUR server, moving it requires technical work. Most clients won't bother.

4. **Annual discount.** Offer 10-15% off for clients who pay annually. This locks in revenue and reduces churn. $149/month × 12 = $1,788. Offer it at $1,499/year (16% discount). You get $1,499 upfront and a guaranteed client for 12 months.

5. **Quarterly check-ins.** A quick 10-minute call every quarter: "Hey, how's the website working for you? Any changes you'd like to make?" This shows you care and surfaces issues before they become cancellations.

## Building the Recurring Revenue Machine

Here's your action plan for building recurring revenue:

Every single client gets a maintenance pitch after website delivery. No exceptions.

As you close more deals, your maintenance base grows. At some point, your recurring revenue will exceed your one-time sales. That's when you know you've built something truly valuable — a business that someone would pay real money to acquire.

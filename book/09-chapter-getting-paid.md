# Chapter 9: Getting Paid (The Right Way)

## Pricing Structures That Maximize Profit

How you price your services can mean the difference between making $2,000 a month and $20,000 a month — even with the same number of clients. Let me walk you through the pricing strategies that work best.

### Strategy 1: Flat Fee (The Simple Approach)

This is the most straightforward pricing model:

| Package | Pages | Features | Price |
|---------|-------|----------|-------|
| Starter | 1-3 pages | Basic site, contact form, mobile | $1,000-$1,500 |
| Professional | 5-7 pages | Full site, gallery, forms, SEO basics | $2,000-$3,500 |
| Premium | 8-12 pages | Everything + blog, booking, advanced features | $4,000-$7,000 |
| E-commerce | Varies | Online store, product listings, payment | $5,000-$10,000 |

**Pros:** Easy to explain, easy to quote, clients know exactly what they're paying.
**Cons:** Leaves money on the table with clients who would pay more.

### Strategy 2: Value-Based Pricing (The Smart Approach)

Instead of pricing based on what it costs you, price based on what it's worth to the client.

A website for a solo dog groomer who makes $3,000/month? Price it at $1,500.
A website for a dental practice that makes $50,000/month? Price it at $5,000-$8,000.

Same amount of work on your end. Wildly different pricing. Why? Because the dentist will get 10x more value from a professional website.

How to figure out value-based pricing:

1. Ask: "What's the average value of a new customer for you?"
2. Ask: "How many new customers per month would make this website worthwhile?"
3. Price your website at 2-3 months' worth of new customer revenue.

Example:
- Dentist says a new patient is worth $2,000/year
- You estimate the website will bring 3-5 new patients per month
- Value to the dentist: $6,000-$10,000 per month
- Your price: $5,000 (he'll make that back in the first month)

### Strategy 3: Flat Fee + Monthly Retainer (The Wealth Builder)

This is the strategy that makes people rich. You charge a one-time fee for the website AND a monthly fee for maintenance, hosting, and updates.

| Component | Price |
|-----------|-------|
| Website build | $2,000-$4,000 |
| Monthly maintenance | $99-$299/month |

The monthly fee covers:
- Website hosting (you pay $5-$15/month, charge $99+)
- Security updates
- Minor content changes (up to X changes per month)
- Uptime monitoring
- Monthly backup

The client gets peace of mind. You get recurring revenue. Everybody wins.

**Why this is the best strategy:** After 12 months with 30 maintenance clients at $149/month, you're making $4,470 per month in recurring revenue — before you close a single new deal. That's $53,640 per year in passive income.

## The 50/50 Payment Method

For one-time website projects, always use the 50/50 split:

- **50% upfront** — Due before any work begins
- **50% on delivery** — Due when the client approves the final website

Why 50/50?

**For you:**
- You get cash in hand before spending anything
- The upfront deposit covers your developer cost and then some
- You're never working "for free" or at risk of non-payment

**For the client:**
- They feel protected — they only pay the rest when they're happy
- It shows you have skin in the game
- It's a fair, professional arrangement

## Contracts, Invoices, and Protecting Yourself

### Simple Contracts

You don't need a lawyer for a basic website contract. Here's what it should include:

> **Website Development Agreement**
>
> **Client:** [Client Name, Business Name]
> **Provider:** [Your Name/Business Name]
> **Date:** [Date]
>
> **Scope of Work:**
> - Design and development of a [X]-page website for [Business Name]
> - Includes: [List pages and features]
> - [X] rounds of revisions included
> - Estimated delivery: [X] business days after deposit received
>
> **Investment:**
> - Total: $[Amount]
> - Deposit (50%): $[Amount] — due before work begins
> - Final payment (50%): $[Amount] — due upon client approval of completed website
>
> **Monthly Maintenance (optional):**
> - $[Amount]/month — includes hosting, updates, security, [X] minor changes/month
> - Billed monthly, can be cancelled with 30 days' notice
>
> **What's Included:**
> - Custom website design
> - Mobile responsive development
> - Contact form setup
> - Basic SEO setup
> - Domain connection
> - 30 days of post-launch support
>
> **What's Not Included:**
> - Logo design (can be added for $[Amount])
> - Copywriting (can be added for $[Amount])
> - Photography (can be added for $[Amount])
> - Ongoing maintenance beyond 30 days (see monthly plan)
>
> **Terms:**
> - Client will provide all content (text, images, logo) within 5 business days of deposit
> - Delays in content delivery may extend the timeline
> - Revisions beyond [X] rounds may incur additional charges
> - Provider retains the right to showcase the website in their portfolio
>
> **Agreement:**
> By paying the deposit, client agrees to the terms above.

Keep it simple. Most small business owners don't want to read a 10-page contract. A clear one-pager builds trust and sets expectations.

### Invoicing

Use one of these free or low-cost invoicing tools:

- **PayPal Invoices** — Free, professional, widely trusted
- **Stripe Invoicing** — Free, modern, great for recurring billing
- **Wave** — Free accounting and invoicing software
- **Square Invoices** — Free, great if clients want to pay by card

Your invoice should include:
- Your business name and contact info
- Client's business name and contact info
- Invoice number and date
- Description of services
- Amount due
- Payment methods accepted
- Due date

### Protecting Yourself

**Rule 1: Never start work without a deposit.** No exceptions. If a client won't put money down, they're not serious.

**Rule 2: Get content upfront or set a deadline.** Many projects stall because the client never sends their photos and text. Set a clear deadline: "Please provide all content within 5 business days of your deposit. Delays in content will push back the delivery date."

**Rule 3: Define revision limits.** Include 2-3 rounds of revisions in your scope. After that, changes are billed at $50-$100/hour. This prevents endless revision cycles.

**Rule 4: Keep records of everything.** Save all emails, messages, files, and payment confirmations. If there's ever a dispute, documentation is your best friend.

**Rule 5: Don't transfer ownership until fully paid.** The website stays on your hosting/staging server until the final payment is received. Only transfer it to the client's domain after full payment. This is your leverage.

## The Monthly Maintenance Goldmine: $99–$299/Month Recurring Revenue

This is the section that can make you wealthy. Seriously. Read it twice.

### Why Monthly Maintenance Is the Real Business

Selling websites for $2,000-$5,000 is great. But it's transactional — you do the work, get paid, and start over. Monthly maintenance is **cumulative** — it grows every single month.

Here's the math:

| Month | New Clients | Maintenance Clients | Monthly Revenue |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | 3 | 2 (of 3 sign up) | $298 |
| 2 | 3 | 4 | $596 |
| 3 | 3 | 6 | $894 |
| 6 | 3 | 12 | $1,788 |
| 12 | 3 | 24 | $3,576 |
| 18 | 3 | 36 | $5,364 |
| 24 | 3 | 48 | $7,152 |

At $149/month per client, after 2 years of closing just 3 deals per month with a 67% maintenance conversion rate, you're making **$7,152 per month in recurring revenue.**

That's $85,824 per year in recurring revenue. Money that comes in whether you work or not. Money that pays your mortgage, your car payment, and your vacations while you sleep.

### What Monthly Maintenance Includes

Your maintenance package should include things that cost you very little but are extremely valuable to the client:

**1. Website Hosting ($5-$15/month actual cost)**
You host their site on a shared hosting account. You pay $5-$15/month. You charge $99-$299/month. This is a huge markup, but the client doesn't know or care what hosting costs — they're paying for the convenience of someone else handling it.

**2. Security Updates**
WordPress sites need regular plugin and theme updates. Takes 5 minutes per site per month.

**3. Monthly Backup**
Most hosting providers do this automatically. You're literally charging for something that happens on its own.

**4. Uptime Monitoring**
Free tools like UptimeRobot alert you if a site goes down. Takes zero effort.

**5. Minor Content Changes**
"Up to 3 small changes per month" — updating a phone number, swapping a photo, changing business hours. Takes 5-10 minutes each.

**6. Monthly Report (Optional for premium plans)**
A simple email: "Your website had [X] visitors this month. Here's what we're working on." Takes 5 minutes with Google Analytics.

### How to Pitch Monthly Maintenance

After delivering the website:

> "Your site is live and looking amazing! Now, I want to make sure it stays that way. Websites need regular maintenance — security updates, backups, and occasional content updates. I offer a monthly maintenance plan for $149/month that covers all of that, plus I'll make up to 3 small changes for you each month. Most of my clients love it because it means they never have to worry about their site. Want me to set that up?"

Most clients will say yes because:
- They just invested $2,000-$5,000 in a website and want to protect that investment
- They don't want to deal with technical maintenance
- $149/month feels small compared to what they just paid
- You've already built trust by delivering a great website

### Pricing Tiers for Monthly Maintenance

| Plan | Price | Includes |
|------|-------|----------|
| Basic | $99/month | Hosting, security updates, monthly backup, 1 content change/month |
| Standard | $149/month | Everything in Basic + 3 content changes/month, uptime monitoring |
| Premium | $249/month | Everything in Standard + priority support, monthly analytics report, unlimited small changes |

Most clients will pick the Standard plan. Some high-value clients (dentists, lawyers, established businesses) will go for Premium.

### Setting Up Recurring Billing

Use one of these tools for automatic monthly billing:

- **Stripe** — Set up recurring invoices; money hits your bank automatically
- **PayPal Subscriptions** — Client subscribes and gets billed monthly
- **GoCardless** — Direct debit billing (great for reliable monthly payments)
- **Wave** — Free recurring invoicing

The key: **automate billing so you never have to chase payments.** Set it up once, and the money flows every month.

## Hiring Your First Virtual Assistant ($3–$5/hour)

The first hire that changes your life isn't a developer or a designer. It's a virtual assistant (VA).

A VA handles the tasks that eat your time but don't directly make money:
- Sending initial outreach emails to new leads
- Following up with leads who didn't respond
- Updating your Excel spreadsheet
- Sending invoices and payment reminders
- Scheduling calls
- Doing basic Google Maps research for new leads

### Where to Find a VA

- **OnlineJobs.ph** — Filipino VAs, $3-$7/hour, excellent English, incredible work ethic
- **Fiverr** — Search "virtual assistant" for project-based help
- **Upwork** — Broader pool, $5-$15/hour depending on experience

### What to Look For

- Excellent written English
- Experience with email, spreadsheets, and basic computer tasks
- Available during your business hours (or close to it)
- Reliable internet connection
- Good communication — responds to messages within hours

### How Much to Pay

For the Philippines (the best value for quality):
- Entry level: $3-$4/hour (part-time, 20 hours/week = $240-$320/month)
- Experienced: $5-$7/hour (full-time, 40 hours/week = $800-$1,120/month)

At $300/month for a part-time VA, you're freeing up 15-20 hours of your time per week. That's 15-20 hours you can spend on sales calls — the activity that actually makes money.

### What to Delegate (And What to Keep)

**Give to your VA:**
- Lead research (Google Maps scraping)
- Initial outreach emails (from templates you create)
- Follow-up emails (from templates)
- Spreadsheet updates
- Invoice sending and payment tracking
- Scheduling appointments
- Basic client communication (status updates)

**Keep for yourself:**
- Sales calls and meetings
- Closing deals and negotiating
- Quality review of finished websites
- Client relationship management
- Strategy and business decisions

### Training Your VA

Don't just hand tasks over and hope for the best. Create simple processes:

1. **Record a Loom video** of yourself doing each task (Loom is free)
2. **Write a simple checklist** for each task
3. **Start with one task at a time** — let them master it before adding more
4. **Review their work daily** for the first two weeks
5. **Give feedback immediately** — don't let mistakes become habits

### The VA ROI Math

Without a VA:
- You spend 2 hours/day on admin tasks + 2 hours on sales = 4 hours total
- You close 3-4 deals/month

With a VA:
- VA handles 2 hours of admin for $6/day
- You spend 4 hours on sales (doubled)
- You close 6-8 deals/month

Extra revenue: 3-4 more deals × $2,500 = $7,500-$10,000/month
VA cost: $300/month
**ROI: 2,500%+**

## Automating Follow-Ups and Proposals

The second growth lever is automation. Not fancy software — just smart templates and systems.

### Email Templates

Create these templates and save them so you (or your VA) can send them in seconds:

**Template 1: Initial Outreach**
Subject, body, everything pre-written. Just swap in the business name and personalization.

**Template 2: First Follow-Up (Day 3)**
A quick bump email for people who didn't respond.

**Template 3: Second Follow-Up (Day 7)**
A different angle — maybe share a portfolio example.

**Template 4: Third Follow-Up (Day 14)**
The "breakup email": "I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right. I'm here whenever you're ready."

**Template 5: Proposal Email**
Pre-formatted with your packages, pricing, and payment terms. Just customize the project details.

**Template 6: Invoice Email**
"Thanks for choosing to work with us! Here's your invoice for the deposit."

**Template 7: Post-Delivery Follow-Up**
"How's the website working out? Any questions?"

**Template 8: Maintenance Pitch**
"Now that your site is live, let's make sure it stays in great shape."

**Template 9: Referral Request**
"Know anyone else who could use a great website?"

### Proposal Template

Create a clean, professional one-page proposal in Google Docs or Canva:

> **Website Proposal for [Business Name]**
>
> Prepared by: [Your Name]
> Date: [Date]
>
> **Understanding Your Needs:**
> [2-3 sentences summarizing what they told you]
>
> **What We'll Build:**
> - [Page 1: Description]
> - [Page 2: Description]
> - [Feature list]
>
> **Examples of Our Work:**
> [3-4 screenshots with brief descriptions]
>
> **Investment:**
> - Option A: [Package name] — $[price]
> - Option B: [Package name] — $[price]
>
> **Timeline:** [X] business days from deposit
>
> **Payment:** 50% deposit to start, 50% on delivery
>
> **Next Step:** Reply "Let's go" and I'll send the invoice!

Having this template ready means you can send a proposal within 30 minutes of a sales call, while the client is still excited.

## Building a Team of Fiverr Developers on Standby

As you scale, one developer won't cut it. You need a bench.

### Your Developer Team Structure

| Role | Number | Rate | Specialty |
|------|--------|------|-----------|
| Primary Developer | 1 | $150-$250 | Your go-to for most projects |
| Backup Developer | 1 | $150-$250 | Same quality, used when primary is busy |
| Budget Developer | 1 | $75-$125 | For simpler, lower-budget projects |
| E-commerce Specialist | 1 | $200-$400 | For online store projects |
| WordPress Expert | 1 | $100-$200 | For WordPress-specific builds |

### How to Build Your Team

1. Start with one great developer
2. Every month, test one new developer with a small project ($100-$150)
3. Rate their quality, speed, and communication
4. Add the good ones to your Developer Roster tab
5. After 3-4 months, you'll have 3-5 trusted developers

### Managing Multiple Projects

When you're juggling 3-5 active projects:

1. **Assign each project to one developer** — never split a project between developers
2. **Stagger project starts** — don't start 5 projects on the same day
3. **Set clear deadlines** — "I need the first draft by [date]"
4. **Check in every 2-3 days** — a quick message keeps them on track
5. **Use your Active Projects tab** — this is your project management system

## The Weekly Routine of a $10K/Month Website Seller

Here's what a typical week looks like when you're making $10K/month:

### Monday: Lead Day
- Review your pipeline in Excel (15 min)
- VA sends outreach emails to new leads (they handle this)
- You make 5-10 sales calls to warm leads (1 hour)
- Follow up on outstanding proposals (30 min)

### Tuesday: Sales Day
- 2-3 scheduled calls or Zoom demos with interested prospects
- Send 1-2 proposals
- VA follows up with cold leads

### Wednesday: Delivery Day
- Review websites in progress with developers
- Present completed websites to clients
- Request and manage revisions
- Collect final payments

### Thursday: Sales Day
- More calls, more proposals
- VA manages leads and follow-ups
- Check in on active projects

### Friday: Admin Day
- Update Revenue Dashboard
- Invoice maintenance clients if needed
- Review the week: leads added, calls made, deals closed
- Plan next week
- Pay your VA and developers

### Saturday-Sunday: Off (or optional hustle)
- Some people work Saturday mornings to get ahead
- Completely optional — the system runs without you on weekends

**Total weekly work: 15-20 hours**

That's a part-time schedule generating $10,000+ per month. And as you add more automation and your VA takes on more responsibility, those hours shrink even further.

- **Not closing enough?** Practice the script. Handle objections better.
- **Delivery issues?** Find better developers. Improve your briefs.

The machine always works. If the output isn't what you want, adjust the input.
